## What the role actually is

Sample listing for local development. You set the mechanical architecture for the next platform generation and defend it against everyone who wants one more degree of freedom. Duty cycle, not peak performance, is the constraint that decides the design.

## What you would work on

- Set the actuator and structural architecture for the next platform, with cost and serviceability as first-order constraints
- Run the design review process that decides what ships and what waits
- Work with manufacturing early enough that DFM is a design input rather than a late surprise

## What they are asking for

- Has taken a mechanical design from concept through volume production, including the parts that went wrong
- Fluent in tolerance analysis and fatigue life, not just CAD
- Can say no to a feature and explain the tradeoff in terms the requester accepts

## Why this one is worth a look

High-mix, low-volume manufacturing is a segment where robots have to be genuinely flexible rather than merely programmable, and the mechanical constraints are correspondingly nasty. This is a staff role with real architectural authority, which is rarer than the title suggests.
