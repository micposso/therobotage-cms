The next robot safety fight in the US will happen state by state.

NVIDIA's new Halos for Robotics matters because most states do not yet have a clear playbook for humanoids, construction robots, or commercial service robots working around people. States have rules for sidewalk delivery robots and autonomous vehicles, but workplace robots still sit inside a patchwork of OSHA, state-plan enforcement, voluntary standards, insurance, and local permitting.

That gap is where Halos gets interesting. Its combination of AI compute, external sensors, outside-in perception, safety software, and inspection could become the kind of evidence states ask for before robots enter construction sites, warehouses, factories, hospitals, and public-facing commercial spaces.

The practical question is no longer whether a robot can perform a task in a demo. It is what the robot can prove to regulators, insurers, workers, and operations teams before it enters someone else's workplace.
