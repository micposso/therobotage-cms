Insurance may matter more than regulation in the next phase of robot deployment.

NVIDIA's new Halos for Robotics matters because most states do not yet have a clear playbook for humanoids, construction robots, or commercial service robots working around people. States have rules for sidewalk delivery robots and autonomous vehicles, but workplace robots still sit inside a patchwork of OSHA, state-plan enforcement, voluntary standards, insurance, and local permitting.

The missing layer is insurance. Workers compensation and commercial general liability carriers already shape workplace safety through underwriting, risk-control guidance, training expectations, claims data, and premium pressure. If robots introduce new bodily injury, property damage, cyber-physical, or product-liability exposure, insurers will ask for proof long before many legislatures write robot-specific rules.

That is where Halos gets interesting. Its combination of AI compute, external sensors, outside-in perception, safety software, and inspection could become the kind of evidence carriers, regulators, workers, and operations teams ask for before robots enter someone else's workplace.
