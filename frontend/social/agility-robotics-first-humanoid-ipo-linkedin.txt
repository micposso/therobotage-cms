The first US humanoid-robot IPO is also the smallest bet in the room.

Agility Robotics is going public at a $2.5 billion valuation under the ticker AGLT — the first company on a US exchange built entirely to make and sell humanoids. It is listing on evidence, not a pitch deck: more than 65,000 operating hours logged by its Digit robot, over $300 million in orders, and live deployments at Schaeffler in South Carolina and GXO in Georgia.

The contrast around it is the real story. Figure AI sits at a $39 billion private valuation on the strength of a single BMW production line. China's Unitree already cleared its Shanghai listing at roughly $6.2 billion — and unlike either US company, it is profitable, shipping more than 5,500 humanoids in 2025 at around 60% margins. Three companies, the same machine, priced from $2.5B to $39B.

The question is no longer whether humanoids can work on a factory floor. It is whether public markets will reward proof over narrative in a year of trillion-dollar AI IPOs.
