Did a robot call offside at the World Cup?

The answer is no if "robot" means a conventional ISO-defined machine that performs locomotion, manipulation, or positioning. The answer gets more interesting if we look at VAR as robotic infrastructure: 12 stadium-mounted cameras, 29 player data points, 50 measurements per second, and a ball sensor transmitting at 500 Hz.

That is the real question the article poses. The football pitch is becoming machine-readable. Cameras become the eyes, the connected ball supplies timing data, software estimates state, and officials become the action layer.

The future of sport may not be robots replacing referees. It may be humans playing inside robotic perception systems.
