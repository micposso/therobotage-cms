VAR forces football to answer a basic robotics question: does a machine need a body to act in the world?

FIFA's semi-automated offside system has no arms, legs, or wheels. But it senses the field through 12 roof cameras, tracks up to 29 points per player at 50 Hz, reads the ball through a 500 Hz sensor, and acts through human referees.

That makes VAR a useful case study in embodied AI without a humanoid body. The system does not replace the referee. It changes what players, coaches, and officials have to comply with: machine precision.

The next generation of footballers may train not only for the offside law, but for the machine's version of the law.
