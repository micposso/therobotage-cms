A robot handover needs two acceptance criteria: the component arrives, and the operator understands what happens next.

Agile ONE SN, scheduled for early 2027, combines a reference humanoid with NVIDIA Isaac GR00T and Jetson Thor. NVIDIA's expanded Isaac tools connect simulation, robot learning and deployment. A shared testbed could give product designers and robotics engineers more time to turn behavior requirements into repeatable experiments.

Consider an interrupted handover: the operator hesitates, then withdraws a hand. What does the robot do? How does it explain the pause? Who authorizes restart? Simulation can help rehearse physical conditions, but understanding those signals still needs testing with intended operators. Planned Halos integration does not establish deployment-specific safety certification.
