THE ROBOT AGE — CONTENT, NAVIGATION AND PROGRAM AUDIT
14 October 2026 | Recommendations only; no production content changed

THE MAIN FINDING
The site currently tells two competing stories. The current /learn page presents an Academy with RPDP and RDP. The homepage, most footers, news sidebars, robot profiles, press materials, and older learning routes still promote REP, RSP, and RXR. A visitor can start on the correct program page and immediately be redirected into the old catalog by shared navigation or promotional content. Fixing shared components is the highest-leverage correction.

Recommended central message:
“The Robot Age prepares people and organizations for a world with robots—through Robot Literacy, hands-on Live Robot Lab experiences, and professional education in robot product design and deployment.”

1. SCOPE AND EVIDENCE
The public sitemap and discovered same-domain links produced 555 URL checks: 550 finished with HTTP 200 and five returned HTTP 404. These are URL checks, not 555 distinct editorial pages: the total includes English and Spanish versions, redirected URLs, and both root URL forms. There are 230 Spanish URLs; the English /jobs family contains 230 URLs, /news contains 35, and /research contains 15 including two error routes.

Every fetched page was extracted into crawl.json and systematically scanned for links, legacy program terms, and status errors. Core marketing/program pages, research essays, shared article promotions, robot profiles, and source components received detailed review. Job listings and repeated translated pages received automated/template-level review. This is not a claim that every employer listing or third-party news assertion has been independently fact-checked. Browser inspection confirmed the rendered Robot Literacy journey. Forms were not submitted; email delivery, checkout, authenticated LMS content, all interactive states, PDFs, and external destination availability were not tested.

The search engine initially returned an obsolete /learn snapshot. The report uses the directly fetched current page and repository source instead. Status results describe this crawl, not a permanent uptime guarantee. Page text excludes navigation/footer for some automated scans; fullText retains the complete extracted document, including content headers.

Evidence files: crawl.json, page-inventory.csv, link-issues.json, and page-review.csv. The last file assigns a recommended role and detected flags to each checked URL. Automated flags are screening results, not a substitute for the detailed findings below.

2. THE FOUR-OFFER MODEL TO USE EVERYWHERE
Robot Literacy | /robot-literacy
Purpose: foundational understanding and responsible participation. Canonical pillars: READ, RELATE, COEXIST. Audience: schools, individuals, educators, organizations, and society. It is the underlying literacy framework; do not silently equate it with an REP credential. Explain which actual teaching formats are available by inquiry.

Live Robot Lab | /live-robot-lab
Purpose: guided, in-person interaction with real robots. Current page: Reachy Mini and Unitree Go2 Pro; initially NYC metropolitan area. Schools from $1,250, universities from $1,500, corporate groups from $2,500; events by quote. Standard school/university format is two hours; corporate formats are 90–120 minutes. State prices as starting prices and requests as inquiries, not confirmed bookings. This is an applied experience, not a professional certification.

RPDP | /learn/rpdp
Public program title: Product Design for Robots. Credential: Robotics Product Design Practitioner. Audience: designers, UX practitioners, design students, researchers, and product managers. Current /learn facts: five weeks, 25 hours, live and self-paced, first cohort planned February 2027; student $149, professional $299. Capstone: Robot Experience Design portfolio project. Current conversion: join the founding cohort list; no seat is reserved.

RDP | /learn/rdp
Public title/credential: Robot Deployment Practitioner. Audience: deployment teams, robotics companies, integrators, operations managers, consultants, and organizational leaders. Current /learn facts: four weeks, 24 hours, commercial launch January 2027. Capstone: Robot Deployment Readiness Plan. Price and delivery schedule are not established on the reviewed page; do not invent them. Use “Get RDP launch updates” until enrollment is actually open.

Both Academy credentials recognize educational completion. Preserve /learn’s clear distinction from government licenses, regulated safety certifications, and authorization to operate robots. Apply the same explanation to credential detail pages. Dates and prices above are the site's current published plans, not independently verified business commitments.

These are four offers, with two professional credential pathways. Avoid “four certifications.” Do not present Robot Literacy or Live Robot Lab as mandatory prerequisites unless the programs actually require them.

Keep existing core URLs. A clearer hierarchy does not require moving every page. Footer groups should be Programs (all four), Research & Tools, Organization (Contact, Partners, Press, confirmed events), and Legal. Remove generic Access from primary discovery; route people to an offer-specific interest form.

The two frameworks need an explicit relationship: Robot Literacy asks people to READ, RELATE, and COEXIST; RXD supplies six dimensions for evaluating a particular robot experience. RES/scorecard is a tool for applying RXD. RPDP uses this design/evaluation material; RDP covers broader organizational deployment planning. Do not use HRF, RXD, RES, and Robot Literacy as interchangeable names.

4. PRIORITY FINDINGS
P0 means repair before the next promotion. P1 means the next content release. P2 means refinement after the core journeys work.

P0 — Competing program catalog [confirmed]
Pages: /, /robot-literacy, /press, /access, /learn/rep, /learn/rsp, /learn/rxr, news sidebars, robot profiles, and default shared footers.
Evidence: homepage “Flagship Certification” promotes REP; /learn lists RPDP and RDP. Even /learn/rdp ends with REP/RPDP/RSP/RXR footer links. /learn itself opts into an Academy-specific footer, so correcting only that page did not update the rest of the site.
Recommendation: make RPDP/RDP the canonical active professional catalog and show all four offers in global program navigation. Remove legacy offers from active promotions. Preserve historical learner access if it exists; mark old pages as archived or superseded. Do not silently relabel an old REP completion as RPDP or assume RSP and RDP are academically equivalent.
Implementation: packages/ui/src/lib/certifications.ts currently has both academyPrograms and a legacy certifications collection. Default shared consumers still use the latter.

P0 — Past cohort dates and misleading reservation language [confirmed]
Pages: /learn/rep and both published robot profiles; shared news reservation modal.
Evidence: May 2026 founding cohort, ten seats, $199 or two payments of $99. The current audit date is October 2026. Two $99 payments total $198. The reservation action opens a waitlist, not a booking confirmation. /access says REP is launching soon while the catalog labels it Live.
Recommendation: remove obsolete promotions; use program-specific interest language. The Academy's “joining the list does not reserve a seat” is the correct model. Do not roll old urgency or seat counts forward without real availability data.
Implementation: FoundingCohort.tsx, WaitlistContext.tsx, NewsArticle/WaitlistModal.tsx, access/page.tsx, and the legacy certification status.

P0 — Wrong curriculum on legacy routes [confirmed]
Pages: /learn/rsp/curriculum and /learn/rxr/curriculum.
Evidence: each uses its own credential label but renders the REP six-week plan and promises an REP credential. RPDP and RDP curriculum URLs correctly redirect to their respective overview #curriculum sections; do not flag those redirects as errors.
Recommendation: retire or properly archive the unsupported legacy curricula. If retained for existing learners, show the actual matching historical syllabus and status.

7. IMPLEMENTATION ORDER AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
First release: centralize active program facts; update homepage/footer/article promotions; remove past-cohort and unsupported reservation copy; repair 404 links and inactive Summit controls; archive or flag unsupported program claims. Correct the arithmetic and contradictory curriculum pages. Decide the truthful status of old credentials without erasing legitimate learner history.

Second release: align Robot Literacy/partners, press biography, enterprise offer, RDP-specific interest handling, Spanish copy, and research evidence. Replace placeholders with real material or omit them. Repair sitemap/search discovery and program-specific confirmations.

Third release: improve editorial routing, browseable archives, scoring explanations, static counter fallbacks, metadata, and measurement. Do not start with a wholesale visual redesign; consistent decisions and destinations matter first.

Use one program record per offer: stable ID, public title, credential name where applicable, audience, outcome, format, duration, start date, status, starting/total price and qualifiers, canonical URL, primary CTA, interest ID, and reviewed-at date. Store historical offerings separately. Generate menus, footer, cards, sitemap, search records, translations, form labels, and confirmation copy from these facts where practical.

Acceptance criteria:
• All active marketing shows the same four-offer model; only RPDP and RDP are presented as current professional credential pathways.
• Every program CTA reaches the promised program, curriculum section, or form.
• No active CTA ends at a 404, placeholder #, inactive button, or unrelated old waitlist.
• No expired cohort is presented as open; no static scarcity or unverified outcome claim remains.
• RPDP/RDP form interest survives submission and confirmation; a list signup never claims a reserved seat.
• Canonical program dates/prices/statuses match across English, Spanish, metadata, shared promotions, and emails.
• Framework names/scales and biographies are consistent; supporting studies are accessible or claims are removed/reframed.
• Sitemap contains published canonical routes, including RDP, and excludes unpublished/error routes and avoidable redirected curriculum URLs.
• Test the main journeys on desktop and mobile, including successful/failed form states, without generating unwanted production leads.

Useful measurements after fixes: program-choice clicks, curriculum views, form starts/completions by program and audience, and misrouted/general-contact inquiries. Keep marketing consent separate from the operational response the person requested.
